About

Macroeconomics, grounded in development research

Genet Zinabou is a Senior Economist at Oxford Economics, where she supports the maintenance and development of the Canadian Provincial-Territorial Model (CPTM), a framework for forecasts, scenarios and impact analysis at the national, provincial and territorial levels. She contributes to consulting work for public- and private-sector clients, covering economic scenario and risk analysis, forecasting and statistical analysis.

Before joining Oxford Economics, she did economic research at the International Monetary Fund, mainly in the Fiscal Affairs Department. There she worked on how fiscal policy affects the climate, on climate adaptation in sub-Saharan Africa, and on gender-responsive budgeting in Bangladesh. She has also worked with ministries of finance, central banks and think tanks on designing economic policy.

Her academic training is in applied microeconomics. Her University of Toronto doctorate, Three Essays in Development and Cultural Economics, covers how values shape entrepreneurship, what China's One Child Policy did to human capital, and whether natural-resource wealth translated into human development in Africa.

Closing Bangladesh's gender gaps could raise output by nearly 40 percent, and women should be treated as drivers of the green transition, not only as beneficiaries of climate action. Core argument of Bangladesh Can Boost Growth and Climate Resilience by Investing in Women, co-written with Jayendu De (IPS News, June 2024)

Academic Research

Three Essays in Development and Cultural Economics

PhD thesis, Graduate Department of Economics, University of Toronto, 2020. Supervisory committee: Daniel Trefler, Nicholas Li and Michel Serafinelli.

Chapter 1 · Culture

Culture and Entrepreneurship: The Role of Obedience as a Moral Value

Cultural groups that treat obedience as a moral value produce fewer entrepreneurs. The relationship appears across language groups within countries, using World Values Survey data. It persists among first- and second-generation immigrants to Canada in the long-form Census. The values come from both parents and the wider community during childhood.

World Values Survey · Canadian Census · epidemiological approach
Chapter 2 · Human capital

Human Capital and the Only-Child Premium: Evidence from China's One Child Policy

With Limin Fang and Tongtong Hao. A difference-in-differences design shows that people who grew up as only children because of the policy got significantly more education than peers with siblings. Early labour-market outcomes changed less. The results fit quantity–quality models in which the effect of family size is non-linear.

Difference-in-differences · IV · China
Chapter 3 · Resources

Natural Resources and Human Development in Sub-Saharan Africa, 1970–2013

Cross-section and panel IV estimates show that resource revenue had no average short-run effect on infant mortality and possibly a negative long-run effect, even as budgets and reported health spending grew. Democratic transitions usually reduce infant mortality, but not in resource-rich nations with weak constraints on power.

Panel IV · political economy · Africa

Policy & Publications

Selected policy work

  • 2024

    Bangladesh Can Boost Growth and Climate Resilience by Investing in Women
    Op-ed with Jayendu De (IMF Resident Representative) · IPS News, also carried by ReliefWeb and The Daily Star coverage

    Argues for skills training, affordable childcare, wider access to formal finance, and bringing gender and climate budget tagging in earlier in the budget process, so that it is more than after-the-fact accounting.

  • 2023

    Women's Empowerment, Gender Budgeting, and Intersection with Climate Change
    IMF Country Report No. 23/410 · Bangladesh: Selected Issues · Lead author, with Jayendu De and Saiful Islam, in collaboration with UN Women

    Examines the macro-criticality of gender equality in Bangladesh, how gender and climate vulnerability intersect (including the effects of male-dominated migration), and how gender-responsive budgeting can advance both goals.

  • 2021

    Monitoring the Climate Impact of Fiscal Policy: Lessons from Tracking the COVID-19 Response
    IMF Working Paper WP/21/259 · with Khaled Eltokhy, Katja Funke, Guohua Huang and Yujin Kim

    Reviews what the various fiscal-policy trackers contributed during the pandemic, assesses the strengths and weaknesses of their methods, and draws lessons for measuring the climate impact of fiscal measures.

  • 2020

    Adapting to Climate Change in Sub-Saharan Africa
    IMF Regional Economic Outlook: Sub-Saharan Africa, April 2020 · Chapter 2, co-author

    Covers the region's high exposure to climate shocks and low adaptive capacity, and how pandemic-recovery spending could also build climate resilience.

  • 2013

    Irrigation and Water Use Efficiency in South Asia
    GDN Working Paper No. 68 · with Ali Hasanain, Shahid Ahmad, Muhammad Zia Mehmood and Sidra Majeed

    Reviews surface and groundwater irrigation policy, water pricing and water markets in South Asia, and the obstacles to adopting water-saving technologies.
